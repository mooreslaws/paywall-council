# Checkout-Funnel Risk Assessment: US Subscription Disclosure Fixture

Assessment date: 2026-07-14
Jurisdictions assessed: United States (federal analysis; state-law coverage and targeting require separate confirmation).

## Assessment Summary

RISK: high
Verdict: The checkout foregrounds a low daily price while placing the materially larger recurring charge and cadence in weaker text before enrollment. Under the stated federal scope, the interface supports a material negative-option disclosure concern pending review of the final billing and consent sequence.

## Material Findings

### F1 - Recurring charge is subordinated to daily pricing

Severity: High
Observed evidence: The selected plan highlights a daily amount, while weaker text states a larger recurring charge every four weeks until cancellation.
Pattern: Hidden renewal; daily-price framing; delayed price disclosure
Risk explanation: The primary commercial impression may leave the consumer without equal prominence for the recurring amount and billing cadence before enrollment.
Applicable norm: Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act, section 4(1), requires clear and conspicuous disclosure of material negative-option terms before billing information is obtained.
Comparable KB authority: FTC v. Vonage, entered court order, addressed recurring enrollment and cancellation practices; the comparison is limited to interface themes and posture.
Linked liability blocks: L1
Confidence and limitations: Medium - The pricing hierarchy is visible, but the final billing screen, consent record, targeting, and state-law facts were not supplied.
Recommended correction: Display the total initial charge, recurring charge, cadence, and cancellation method with equal prominence before billing information and again at final authorization.
