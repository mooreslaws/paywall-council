# Cumulative Assessment

Read this whenever a submission shows several small practices rather than one obvious
violation, and whenever a risk label is being set.

## The question

May several individually minor interface practices be treated together as one more
serious matter? Yes for the risk assessment, and in the UK yes for the finding itself.
The grounding differs by jurisdiction, and the difference matters.

## What the sources actually support

**US.** The FTC's Deception Policy Statement commits the Commission to evaluate "the
entire advertisement, transaction, or course of dealing", examining "the entire mosaic,
rather than each tile". The .com Disclosures guidance repeats this for online screens:
advertisers "should not focus only on individual phrases or statements, but should
consider the ad as a whole". The 2022 staff report Bringing Dark Patterns to Light states
that dark patterns "often are not used in isolation and tend to have even stronger effects
when they are combined". Together these support reading a checkout as a whole. They are
policy and guidance, not a statutory head of liability, so they inform how a Section 5(a)
deception theory is framed rather than creating one.

**EU.** Article 6(1) UCPD puts this inside the misleading-action test itself: a practice
is misleading if it "in any way, including overall presentation, deceives or is likely to
deceive the average consumer, even if the information is factually correct". The
Commission's UCPD Guidance explains that Article 6 covers presentation precisely because
how information is presented drives consumer response. The Commission's own behavioural
study found that "unfair commercial practices are rarely presented in isolation" and that
each site reviewed used several dark patterns at once.

**UK.** The strongest position of the three, because it is codified. Section 226 DMCC Act
2024 makes "an overall presentation which is likely to deceive the average consumer" a
standalone head of misleading action, and says expressly that an overall presentation may
deceive even where the information conveyed is correct. CMA207 reads this as covering
information that "is not actually false, but is arranged, displayed or otherwise presented
in a way that is likely to deceive". The CMA's online choice architecture work states that
these practices "tend to have stronger effects when they are combined" and that "harm may
be aggravated where practices occur in combination".

## Where the support stops

Every source above is about the OVERALL PRESENTATION of one funnel, or about combination
as an aggravating factor. None of them says that unrelated minor practices may be summed
until they cross a threshold. The distinction is the whole point:

- Permitted: several practices that together shape one deceptive impression of the same
  matter (the real price, the real commitment, the real ease of leaving) are assessed
  together, and in the UK that combined presentation is itself the misleading action.
- Not permitted: adding up practices that mislead about nothing in particular, or that no
  norm reaches, in order to manufacture a violation.

A pattern that no routed norm reaches contributes to the risk label at most as context. It
never becomes a Material Finding and it never acquires a citation by being counted
alongside others.

## Applying it

1. Decide what impression the combination creates, and about which matter relevant to a
   transactional decision. Name it. If no single matter emerges, there is no cumulative
   case and the practices stay separate.
2. Cite the combination only where a routed norm supports it. In the UK that is Section
   226; in the EU the Article 6 overall-presentation limb; in the US a Section 5(a)
   deception theory framed on net impression.
3. Say plainly in the finding that the conclusion rests on the combination rather than on
   any single element, so a reader can disagree with the aggregation on its own terms.
4. Where the parts are individually minor and the combination is the point, the finding
   carries at most Medium severity unless the combined impression concerns price, the
   existence of a recurring commitment, or the ability to cancel.
